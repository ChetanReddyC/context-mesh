# Code of Conduct

This project adopts the Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct as its standard. The full text is available at <https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/>.

The short version, in our own words:

---

## Be Honest

- Disagreement is welcome. Disrespect is not.
- Critique work, not people.
- Acknowledge when you're wrong. Update your priors when shown evidence.
- Don't perform certainty you don't feel.

## Be Specific

- Vague complaints help no one. Concrete observations move the work forward.
- "This is bad" is noise. "This breaks invariant X because of Y" is signal.
- Same goes for praise — "great PR" is forgettable; "this approach to Z is sharp because A" sticks.

## Be Kind

- Engineers are not their code. A bad review of code is fine; a bad review of a person is not.
- Newcomers ask basic questions because they're new, not because they're stupid.
- Patience and clear explanations cost little and earn a lot.

## Be Inclusive

- Everyone in this project came from somewhere different. Different backgrounds, different experience levels, different first languages.
- Use inclusive language. Avoid jargon when plainer words exist. Translate when something isn't obvious.

---

## Unacceptable Behavior

- Personal attacks, harassment, threats, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory language or imagery related to identity (race, gender, religion, etc.).
- Publishing others' private information without consent.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

---

## Enforcement

Violations are addressed by maintainers privately first. Repeated or severe violations may result in:

1. A private warning with a request to change behavior.
2. Temporary suspension from the project's communication channels.
3. Permanent removal from the project.

Maintainers act in good faith, transparently, and consistently.

---

## Reporting

Report violations privately to the project's maintainer (currently Chetan). Reports are kept confidential.

---

## Final Word

This is a small but real community. The norms here aren't bureaucracy — they're how we make working together pleasant and productive. We expect everyone to take them seriously, including ourselves.
